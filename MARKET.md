# Market Analysis: Magnus (book swap + book club community)

*Researched 2026-10-30. Sources are linked at the bottom. Some data is old,
and that's flagged where it matters. Treat every number as something to
re-check in user interviews, not as truth.*

---

## TL;DR

1. **People want to read socially, and the demand is real in both countries.**
   Malaysia's silent reading groups went from 4 people to ~100 per session.
   In Seoul, a 10,000-member reading club filled in under 2 hours.
2. **Organizers are underserved.** Almost every club runs on Instagram +
   WhatsApp/Telegram/Facebook, with no RSVP, waitlist, reminders or member
   history. That's the gap.
3. **Swapping alone is a weak business.** It's infrequent, needs a dense
   local network, and past swap apps stayed small (KL's own oneforone, 2017;
   Browzly in Dubai, ~400 members). Swapping works as a **feature of
   gatherings**, not as the product.
4. **Positioning that fits the evidence:**
   *"The home for book clubs: events, RSVPs and a swap table, all in one
   link."* Win the organizers and they bring their members.
5. **Money is modest.** A realistic Klang Valley beachhead is side-income
   scale (around RM2k/month at maturity; see the math below), which fits
   "portfolio first, revenue second".

## 1. Malaysia (primary market)

### Reading demand
| Signal | Number | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Avg books read per year (PNM *Reading Profile* 2022/23) | **24** (≈16 print, 8 digital), up from 2 in 2005 and 15 in 2015 | Self-reported. Critics question it. |
| Share of "active readers" | **88.6%** | Same study, same caveat |
| KL International Book Fair 2025 visitors | **1.8 million** | Fair attendance ≠ reading |
| Imported book prices in KL | RM60-115 for one book (~3.5-7% of RM1,700 minimum wage) | One writer's examples |

**Read:** demand is high and books are expensive. That's why Big Bad Wolf
(up to 95% off), BookXcess (remainders, 8 → 19 stores by 2022) and
secondhand/Carousell exist. **Cheaper access to books** is a real
motivation. Swapping fits it.

### The community scene (where the users are today)
| Group | What it is | Size / signal | Runs on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kuala Lumpur Reads** (since 2023) | Silent reading in Perdana Botanical Garden, Saturdays. Chapters in Cheras and Cyberjaya. | ~100 people/session (from 4-5); Cyberjaya went 20 → 60-70 in its second week | Social media / word of mouth |
| **KL Book Exchange** (since 2014) | Offline book swaps, free | Facebook group of ~4,000 (per earlier research) | Facebook |
| Eat Books Club, Flipside Social Club, Rish's Book Club, The Badass Book Club | Café meetups, silent reading parties, themed monthly clubs | Featured by Tatler | Instagram |
| Women's reading club (gov't, 2026) | National reading-culture push | Policy tailwind | n/a |

**Lessons from history:** KL Book Exchange's founder launched **oneforone**
(online swap, 2017). It had ~300 books in beta, RM16k setup cost, sales
covered ~20% of costs, and was run solo. An earlier rental idea failed for
lack of publicity. **Distribution and cost killed it, not the idea.** The
PWA + free tiers fix cost. Organizer partnerships have to fix distribution.

### Competitors / alternatives in Malaysia
| Alternative | Strength | Weakness Magnus can use |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram + WhatsApp/Telegram | Free, everyone's already there | No RSVP/waitlist, no reminders, events get lost in feeds |
| Facebook groups (KL Book Exchange) | Big audience | Aging platform for Gen Z, swaps buried in posts |
| Carousell / Shopee | Huge reach, payments | Selling, not community; narrow book selection |
| Bookurve (trade-in, rental) | Business already buying used books | Retail, not peer-to-peer or social |
| Big Bad Wolf, BookXcess | Very cheap new books | Seasonal / retail, no community |
| Goodreads, StoryGraph, Fable | Tracking + online clubs | Not local, not in-person |

There's **no local app for in-person book clubs plus swapping.** That matches
the earlier finding.

## 2. South Korea (secondary, and where Zarith lives)

### Reading demand: split by age
| Signal | Number |
|---|---|
| Adult reading rate (2025 national survey) | **38.5%**, a record low; 2.4 books/year |
| People in their 20s | **75.3%** and rising (the "text hip" trend) |
| E-books vs print, 20s | **59.4% e-book** vs 45.1% print |
| Seoul International Book Fair 2025 | 150k+ visitors, tickets sold out |
| Seoul's *Hip Dok Club* (2025) | **10,000 slots filled in < 2 hours**; gamified (points, levels, merch) |

**Read:** Young Koreans read to be social and to share. But they lean toward
e-books, so swapping **physical** books is a weaker draw than in Malaysia.
The appeal here is the **club and the identity**.

### Willingness to pay: Trevari
Paid book-club company: **₩190k-290k per 4 months**, 70%+ re-enrollment,
profitable without outside funding (2016-17 reports; old data, needs a
re-check). This shows Koreans will **pay for well-run clubs**. It's
also a strong, established competitor, along with Munto (social hobby
meetups), Somoim, and Daangn's local groups. Aladin's used bookstores make
selling used books easy, which weakens the case for swapping.

### The niche that fits you: English readers in Korea
**Seoul Silent Book Club** (run by two Americans) reports that expats
struggle to find welcoming Korean clubs, many Seoul clubs **charge fees or
are selective**, and venues for big groups are hard to find. They already
**run book swaps** (Gwanghwamun) and organize through Instagram + Facebook.
English books are hard to find in Korea too (dbBOOKS warehouse sales and
Tongbang draw crowds).

→ A **bilingual, foreigner-friendly** wedge (international students at
Hanyang ERICA, Seoul expats) is underserved, and you can test it in person.

## 3. Global comparables

| Company | Model | Outcome | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fable** | Social reading app, online clubs, premium tier, bookstore | ~3M users, 100k+ clubs → **acquired by Everand (Scribd), June 2025** | Clubs-as-product can be worth real money |
| **Silent Book Club** | Volunteer-led IRL chapters | **2,000+ chapters, 60+ countries** (2026) | The IRL format is spreading. Organizers need light tools. |
| **Trevari** (KR) | Paid, curated clubs | Profitable, high retention (old data) | People pay for curation + a good experience |
| **Browzly** (UAE) | Swap credits ("Swids"), courier option | ~400 members early (2018) | Credits beat strict 1-for-1. Still small. |
| BookMooch / PaperbackSwap | Points-based postal swapping | Long-lived but niche | Swapping alone stays niche |
| oneforone (MY) | Online swap, pay postage | Struggled (2017) | Distribution + cost |

## 4. Where Magnus fits

**Users (two sides):**
1. **Organizers** (supply). Run 1-4 events/month for 10-100 people. Their
   pain: chasing RSVPs, no-shows, venue headcount, announcing in 3 apps,
   collecting fees by DuitNow/bank transfer screenshots.
2. **Readers** (demand). Their pain: finding a club that fits, books are
   expensive, they want reading friends. Swapping is a bonus that saves money.

**Wedge:** Free tools for organizers → each club brings its members →
swapping happens at the meetups people already attend.

### Feature ideas from the research
| Idea | Why the evidence supports it |
|---|---|
| **Event page + RSVP + waitlist + reminders**, shareable to WhatsApp/IG | The #1 organizer gap. Link-first growth (see ADR 001) |
| **Swap table at events**: list the books you'll bring, others reserve them before the meetup | Fixes swapping's density and trust problems: you meet at a public event, no couriers |
| **Swap credits** (give 1 → earn 1 credit), not strict 1-for-1 | Browzly/BookMooch pattern. No need for perfect matches. |
| Multi-party swap cycles (graph matching) | Portfolio depth. Better as **v2** on top of credits |
| Club discovery map ("clubs near me, this weekend") | Readers can't find clubs today |
| Paid event ticketing (FPX/DuitNow; Toss/Kakao Pay in KR) | Main revenue. Trevari proves people pay. |
| Reading streaks / badges | Hip Dok Club filled 10k slots with gamification. Later. |
| Bilingual EN/BM/KR | Fits expat clubs in Korea and Malaysia's mix of languages |

### Money: honest math (illustrative assumptions)
30 clubs × 2 paid events/month × 20 attendees × RM30 ticket = **RM36,000
gross ticket sales/month** → at a 5% fee = **~RM1,800/month**.
Add-ons (organizer Pro tier, café partner listings, affiliate links to
BookXcess/Shopee) could double that. That's side income, not a venture-scale
business. Fine for a portfolio project. Growing beyond that means more
cities or clubs as a service (the Trevari model).

## 5. Risks
1. **Organizers won't switch** from Instagram/WhatsApp. *Mitigation:* don't
   replace them. Make the event link something they *post* on IG/WhatsApp.
2. **Free clubs resist fees.** *Mitigation:* free for free events; charge
   only on paid tickets.
3. **Swap density / trust.** *Mitigation:* swaps happen at events only (v1).
4. **Distance:** the main market is KL, but you're in Ansan.
   *Mitigation:* remote interviews with KL organizers, plus an in-person
   pilot at ERICA / with Seoul expat clubs.
5. **History:** oneforone struggled. *Mitigation:* near-zero costs, and partners
   before launch.

---

## 6. Validation plan (before writing code)
**Talk to 5 organizers + 5 readers** (KL Reads chapters, Flipside, Rish's,
Badass Book Club; Seoul Silent Book Club; ERICA international students).

Ask organizers:
- Walk me through organizing your last event, from idea to the day after.
- What's the most annoying part? What tools do you use?
- How do you handle RSVPs, no-shows, and fees?
- Have you tried a tool for this (Meetup, Luma, Eventbrite, Google Forms)? Why did you stop?
- Would you try a free event page next month if I set it up for you?

Ask readers:
- How do you find book clubs? Last club you tried, and why did you stay or leave?
- Where did your last 5 books come from? What did they cost?
- Have you ever swapped books? What made it easy or hard?

**Success signal:** at least **2 organizers agree to run a real event on
Magnus** (a commitment, not just "sounds cool").
